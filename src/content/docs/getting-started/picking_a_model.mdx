## Picking the right model for the task
When you're picking a model, you need to know the task. You'd be surprised how often
you can get away with a dirt cheap model!

For your "main model", the one used to generate the response you see on screen, a
more powerful model is often preferred. These models may need to follow complex
instructions, call multiple tools or dive deep into the psychology of your characters.
Cheaper models simply don't do this very well. The common sentiment regarding LLMs for
prose is that quality scales fairly linearly with cost, so if you want high quality
prose, you'll likely need a high quality model.

For other tasks where you don't need colorful language, complex instructions, among
other things, you can get away with a dirt cheap model. Think tasks such as summarizing
the story, calling a single tool, or other similar tasks. Cheap models do these just fine.
